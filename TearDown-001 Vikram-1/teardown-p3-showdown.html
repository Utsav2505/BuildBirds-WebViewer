THE LEAP · 3D-PRINTED ENGINES2 of 2
Instead of casting and machining metal parts, a printer builds the whole engine in metal, layer by layer.
  • Months → days. A cast-and-machined engine took months; a printed one is done in days.
  • ~50% lighter, far fewer parts — printing makes shapes machining simply can't.
  • Whole engines, printed in under a week — India's AgniKul printed its Agnilet engine in under 4 days; Rocket Lab has printed 1,000+.
Vikram-1's Raman-1 is India's first fully 3D-printed orbital engine.
